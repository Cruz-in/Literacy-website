# Imagery Website — Project Memory

## Project Goal
Build a scroll-driven single-page site that teaches the literary device "imagery" by immersing the reader in a sensory walk through a forest at dusk.

## Themed Concept
**Setting:** Forest trail at dusk  
**Medium:** Scroll-driven single-page site  
**UX Logic:** Tell → Show → Prove → Cite → Close

## Six-Section Scroll Order
1. **Hero** — "imagery." headline in the sky behind the treeline, hook ("Language you don't just read — you feel"), credit, scroll cue
2. **Definition** — "WHAT IS IMAGERY" teaching section with own-words definition
3. **The Five Senses** — 5 full-height immersive sections (Sight, Sound, Smell, Touch, Taste) with sensory prose
4. **Original Example** — Student-written paragraph weaving all five senses together
5. **Real-World Example** — Robert Frost citation with analysis
6. **Closing** — Closing reflection line + student credit (name, class, date)

## Contrast-Weight System (Core Design Rule)
Two weights guide the site's wayfinding:

- **SOFT / Gradual Contrast** → Sense sections (3)  
  Forest-app style: type close in value to color world, subtle glow, immersive/dreamlike. **Contrast carries the mood.**

- **HARD / High Contrast** → Teaching sections (2, 4, 5, 6)  
  Sukoya style: bright heavy text on solid dark, reads clearly. **Contrast carries information.**

The weight itself becomes navigation: *soft = experience, sharp = information.*

## Animation Ground Rules
- Animate `transform`/`opacity` only where possible. Use `requestAnimationFrame` for JS loops.
- Pause rendering when it can't be seen: IntersectionObserver for off-screen elements, `document.hidden` for background tabs.
- `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` gives a still version of the effect (static grain, a few still motes), or nothing. Never a broken one.
- Effects sit above the imagery and below the text. They never reduce legibility.

## Particle Module (`particles.js`)
`particles(canvas, options)` draws soft motes drifting on any canvas. It's a classic script (not an ES module, because `file://` blocks those), loaded with `defer`.
- **Options** (defaults = the page dust): `count` 55, `stillCount` 12 (shown still under reduced motion; 0 = none), `size` [1, 3] px back→front, `opacity` [0.12, 0.5] back→front, `speed` 14 px/s (front tier; the back tier moves at 30%), `drift` [0.25, -1] (direction x, y; up is negative), `wander` 0.6 (sideways sway ÷ speed), `twinkle` 0 (opacity flicker depth 0–1; each mote has its own 1.5–4s rhythm), `repel` 0 (px: motes inside this radius of the pointer are pushed straight out, at `repel × 1.6` px/s at the center falling to 0 at the rim; mouse and pen only, and nothing under reduced motion because the loop isn't running), `tiers` 3, `color` `"255, 238, 214"` (rgb string).
- **Inside:** each tier's size, opacity and speed come from where it sits between back and front (±20% jitter). Back motes outnumber front ones. Each mote sways across its drift line on two slow sines and wraps one mote-width past the edges. Positions are stored as fractions of the canvas, so a resize never empties the field. One soft-dot sprite is pre-drawn and stamped for every mote. `dt` is clamped to 50 ms. One `sync()` starts or stops the loop on IntersectionObserver, `visibilitychange` and reduced-motion `change`. A ResizeObserver sizes the canvas to its CSS box × DPR.
- **Reuse in a section:** put a `<canvas>` in the section **markup** (not added by JS: the reveal observer only picks up `section > *` that exist at DOMContentLoaded, and a child added later stays at opacity 0). Give it `position: absolute; width: 100%; height: 100%; pointer-events: none; z-index: 2`. `section > *` is z 4 (text), so z 2 keeps it under the film and the text. Then call `particles(canvas, {...})`. Its IntersectionObserver pauses it off-screen, for free. Used by SIGHT (`.light-motes`), SMELL (`.spores`), TASTE (`.shimmer`) and the fireflies (`.fireflies`, in the hero and three senses). Remember `width: 100%; height: 100%` on the canvas: `inset: 0` alone leaves it at 300×150.

**Just Completed:** The journey thread — the page's signature wayfinding. The rail's short trail is gone; one line now winds the whole page, woven through the gaps in the actual layout, drawing itself behind you.

- **Markup:** `<div class="thread">` is appended to `<body>` by the `<head>` script, holding one `<svg>` (3 paths: the faint whole walk, a wide soft bloom, the hairline) and one `<i>` (the firefly head). z 2 + `mix-blend-mode: screen`, like every other light on the page: over the photos and section colors, under the film (3) and the words (4). It's a body child, so `section { overflow: hidden }` never clips it. The svg is sized in px (`viewBox="0 0 W H"`, `width`/`height` = the same) so userspace = px and stroke widths are literal; `overflow: hidden` on it stops the stroke spilling past the page and growing the document on each rebuild.
- **Waypoints come from the layout, never hardcoded.** `box()` walks the `offsetParent` chain rather than using `getBoundingClientRect` — **this is the whole trick**: the reveal translates every section child 2.5rem and Taste's label -50%, so a rect measures the animation, not the layout. The thread is rebuilt on resize (debounced 160ms) and on `document.fonts.ready` (Fraunces and Jost shift every height).
- **How it weaves, in four rules:**
  1. `gaps(y)` returns the clear stretches of a *row*, from every text block on the page at once — not one element at a time. Checking the whole row is what keeps it off a block's **neighbour**: the hero's hook and its facts share a row, and "to the right of the hook" is on top of the facts.
  2. `place()` picks the gap **nearest the lane it's already walking** and leans `SWAY` (110px) to the alternating side of it. Measuring from where the line already is, rather than from the gap's middle, is what stops it swinging the full width of an empty row and back — that one change took the path from 1.95× the page height (a scribble) to 1.2× (a trail).
  3. The elements are visited **sorted by y**, not in document order: Touch's and Taste's quotes sit *above* their labels, and in document order the spacing filter silently dropped both.
  4. `cuts()` is an exact segment-vs-rect test (exact because the line only ever descends, so its x at a block's top and bottom row bounds every x between). If a run would clip a block, `step()` either crosses in the clear band **above** it, slides the previous waypoint into this lane, or carries straight down past its **foot** and crosses under.
- **Counters are obstacles, not anchors** (`BLOCKS` includes `.count`, `KEY` doesn't). Anchoring to one boxed the thread into the wrong lane 10px above a quote it then had to cross — unsolvable geometry for a 0.75rem label not worth visiting.
- **Draw-on-scroll:** `--len` is the path's length in px, measured once per build; `--p` is the fraction walked. `stroke-dashoffset: calc(var(--len) * (1 - var(--p)))`. A 320-sample length→y table is built once (forced monotonic, so a binary search is valid), so the scroll handler does **no geometry calls at all** — one lookup, two style writes, one rAF per scroll burst. The target is `scrollY + vh * (0.55 + 0.45 * progress)`: the line trails behind you and still finishes exactly drawn at the foot of the page, with no special case.
- **Color: solid `--accent` the whole way down.** It was built as a `userSpaceOnUse` gradient shifting through each world (gold → amber → green → cold grey → deep red); Cruz saw the orange at the top of the page and asked for that the whole way, so the gradient, its stops and the `worlds` table are gone. One accent, like the rest of the site.

Base `display: none` + no dash, so **reduced motion gets the whole thread, still, and no firefly** — never a blank page or a half-drawn line. The scroll handler still runs and sets the vars; the CSS decides they mean nothing.
